Topic A1 · The analyst

What makes a good analyst?

  • Flexibility adapt to changing circumstances
  • Logical and independent mind question assumptions and reason clearly
  • Attention to detail the small clues carry the investigation
  • Objectivity and sensitivity handle confidential information with care
  • Concentration and patience long analysis units, careful work
  • Confidence in your own judgement defensible conclusions

Topic A1 · The analyst

Analyst bias

  • Conscious and unconscious bias built through upbringing, culture, education and occupation
  • Cognitive bias systematic errors in thinking that distort judgement
  • Forensic confirmation bias pre-existing beliefs and motives shape how evidence is gathered
  • The risk prejudicial information — ethnicity, criminal record — compromises impartiality
  • The guard recognise and actively counter bias in your analysis

Topic A1 · IR lifecycle

Benefits and utility of incident response

  • A framework for incidents reduces errors and prevents loss of critical forensic data
  • A clear path to the cause establishes how the incident happened
  • Legal and stakeholder notification all parties informed when required
  • Reduced reputational damage effective management of the incident
  • Rapid, systematic recovery business impact kept to a minimum
Topic A1 · IR lifecycle

Preparation before an incident

  • Establish an IR team roles and responsibilities agreed in advance
  • Conduct threat analysis know the likely threat actors
  • Outline response guidelines and procedures for external communication
  • Communicate with personnel and understand the relevant policies
  • Train employees and test the IR plan
  • Capture lessons learned improve the plan after every exercise
Topic A1 · IR lifecycle

The IR lifecycle

  1. Suspect a compromise
    human or machine detection of an anomaly
  2. Acquire data
    memory dumps, packet captures, logs, disk images
  3. Analyse data
    correlate and reconstruct the timeline
  4. Report
    a forensic, structured summary of the compromise
  5. Deploy countermeasures
    prevent recurrence and harden the posture
Topic A1 · Kill chain

The cyber kill chain

  1. Reconnaissance
    research, identify and select targets
  2. Weaponisation
    pair remote-access malware with an exploit into a deliverable
  3. Delivery
    transmit the weapon to the target
  4. Exploitation
    the weapon executes and runs code
  5. Installation
    a backdoor is installed for persistence
  6. Command and control
    the code connects back for instructions
  7. Actions on objective
    the attacker pursues their aims

Activity: map the kill chain

  • A user opens a PDF that drops a backdoor — which kill chain phase?
  • The malware phones home for instructions — which phase?
  • The attacker pivots to steal data — which phase?
  • A spear-phishing email is crafted for a named victim — which phase?
  • Order the seven phases for a ransomware intrusion.
Topic A2 · Chronology

Incident chronology

  • Reconstruct the timeline order events from all evidence sources
  • Correlate sources network logs, host logs, captures, memory
  • Establish a chain of events what happened, when, and in what order
  • Feed the bigger picture cross-reference with historical reports
  • Enable management metrics and shared threat intelligence
Topic A3 · Law & compliance

Law, compliance and evidence

  • Engagement lifecycle scope, legality and record-keeping
  • Applicable legislation Computer Misuse Act, Data Protection Act, GDPR
  • Evidence integrity do not change data; preserve the audit trail
  • Independent review a third party should reproduce your result
  • Confidentiality sensitive data handled appropriately
Topic A5 · Threat assessment

Risk ownership and the TAME model

  • Risk ownership each risk is owned by a named stakeholder
  • Documentation supports ISO 27001, ISO 9001 and other certifications
  • TAME model Threat Assessment Model for Electronic payment systems
  • Four stages assessment scope; threat agent & vulnerability analysis; scenario construction & system modelling; evaluation
  • Scalable built for payment systems, extensible to enterprise networks
